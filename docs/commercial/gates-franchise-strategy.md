# Gates Franchise Strategy — Compact Games, Compounding Catalog

> **Working commercial direction.** This is a planning document, not a release promise. It records the intended business shape around Second Gate and future Gate titles so that Second Gate does not expand merely to carry every commercial ambition by itself.

## Thesis

Second Gate should be treated as the **first released entry in a continuing body of compact, authored Gate games**, not as one enormous RPG that must amortize every year of Thestra development by itself.

The commercial objective is therefore broader than maximizing Second Gate's individual content volume:

> **Ship a dense, complete paid game that establishes an audience, a reusable production pipeline, and a catalog that becomes more valuable with every later Gate.**

This strategy turns reusable Thestra work into production leverage. Runtime, Studio, renderer, battle, eventing, export, controller, save, UI, and authoring improvements should make the second and third Gate materially cheaper to make than the first.

## Second Gate launch model

Current preferred launch direction:

- paid base game rather than free-to-play or a free base supported primarily by extras;
- **$9.99 USD** as the current working launch-price hypothesis, subject to store testing, final scope, regional pricing, and market conditions;
- compact enough that the price does not pressure the project into filler;
- complete at launch, with no requirement that paid content DLC finish the story;
- optional soundtrack and art/publication products may add revenue without gating game content.

A free Second Gate remains a possible **later catalog strategy** after multiple paid Gate titles exist. At that point making an older entry free could rationally acquire players for a larger back catalog. It is not the preferred initial monetization model.

## Density over defensive scope

Do not add content merely to justify a higher price or conform to an imagined JRPG hour count.

The desired relationship is:

- fewer but memorable locations over filler geography;
- fewer but individually legible creatures over roster inflation;
- consequential returns and town reactions over large quantities of generic dialogue;
- dungeon floors with distinct identity, decisions, and discoveries over floor count for its own sake.

Second Gate's owner direction is nevertheless **not** a three-floor microgame. Current dungeon-scale intent is:

- **9 floors:** bare minimum;
- **12 floors:** comfortable working target;
- **15 floors:** desirable if authoring throughput supports equal density rather than dilution.

Do not market a final floor count until production evidence justifies locking it.

- **Steam Next Fest: February 22–March 1, 2027 — intended target.**

Official Steamworks schedule and requirements are time-sensitive and must be rechecked before acting. As of August 2026, Valve lists:

- February 8, 2027: required review-submission deadline;
- February 11, 2027: press preview begins;
- February 22–March 1, 2027: Next Fest.

Source: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027

Operationally, this means **January 2027 should be a demo-polish/review month, not the month in which the demo's game design is still being invented.**

Do not set the final commercial launch date solely to satisfy Next Fest. Launch after the festival when the complete game and release operations are ready.

Publish the Second Gate Coming Soon page once the public title, capsule direction, screenshots, and core promise are representative enough that public discussion should convert into wishlists.

Do not wait for the entire public demo merely because older planning treated the store page as a later stage.

## The next Gate

The next commercial beat after Second Gate should not default to an indefinite silence followed by an unrelated future project.

Preferred direction:

- let Second Gate launch and receive its immediate support window first;
- roughly **1–3 months after release**, if a credible next project exists, announce the next Gate and publish its Coming Soon page;
- this is a **store-page / audience handoff target**, not a promise to ship another full game 1–3 months later;
- the next title may be First Gate, Seventh Gate, or another numbered Gate depending on the actual work.

The numbering need not be chronological. Beginning the released corpus with **Second Gate** and later producing **First Gate** can itself create productive mystery.

## Speculative Gate forms — idea reservoir, not roadmap

The Gate names below are **non-binding possibilities**. They exist to preserve mechanically fertile directions and to give good ideas somewhere to go without expanding Second Gate. They do not commit the repository to a release order, feature set, production schedule, or even the eventual existence of each title.

- **Seventh Gate** — a plausible direct mechanical follow-up to Second Gate: another first-person dungeon crawler centered on commanding monsters, deliberately familiar in setup but more refined through the systems, tools, and production knowledge earned by Second Gate. Its value is continuity and iteration rather than mandatory reinvention.
- **First Gate** — a plausible linear tactical RPG and likely the first Gate built around a named protagonist. The useful **Brigandine** reference is primarily its combat grammar — commanders/summoners organizing monster retinues and tactical formations — rather than its territorial grand-strategy map. **Kartia: The World of Fate** is a complementary reference for a strongly authored linear story and for treating summoning/created units as part of the battle system itself. This does not presently imply a conquest-map game.
- **Fifth Gate** — intentionally undefined. Keeping an unassigned Gate available is itself useful: not every future mechanic, setting, or genre experiment needs to be forced into an already imagined sequel.
- **Zeroth Gate** — a possible later science-fiction departure, intentionally stranger in tone and framing than the other currently imagined Gates. The slightly awkward **Zeroth** ordinal is part of the appeal rather than something to normalize into a conventional sequel number.

These possibilities also reinforce that Gate numbers are better understood as **identities/events within the corpus, not installment numbers**. A release sequence may jump between Second, Seventh, First, Fifth, Zeroth, or other Gates without needing numerical order.

Do not turn this section into a speculative backlog. In particular:

- do not open implementation issues for a future Gate merely because an idea is recorded here;
- do not generalize Thestra pre-emptively around an imagined future title unless the current game already provides evidence that the abstraction is useful;
- do not move a mechanic into Second Gate merely because its eventual home is uncertain;
- allow future Gate concepts to change substantially once they become real projects.

The productive function of franchise speculation is **scope release**: a promising idea can remain alive without becoming a requirement for the current game.

## Catalog flywheel

The desired long-term loop is:

1. a new Gate attracts players;
2. the game's ending/support cycle points toward another Gate;
3. the next Gate's page captures part of that audience before attention dissipates;
4. franchise/catalog surfaces expose earlier games to later players;
5. recurring motifs and optional encounters make earlier entries artistically interesting to revisit;
6. each game amortizes more of the same Thestra infrastructure.

The strategic asset is therefore not only one launch. It is **a recognizable body of work with accumulating back-catalog value**.

## Corpus recurrence, not continuity bookkeeping

Future Gate titles may reuse the creator's existing corpus in the spirit of recurring tri-Ace figures, superbosses, motifs, items, places, and impossible guest appearances.

This does **not** require one perfectly reconciled timeline.

Useful forms of recurrence include:

- a returning being or name in a different role;
- a recurring optional superboss;
- a place or image reappearing under changed circumstances;
- a mechanical idea reinterpreted by another game;
- a musical cell or battle motif returning;
- an impossible guest encounter from another Gate;
- a character appearing before the game that properly introduces them exists.

Contradiction may remain productive. A recurrent figure need not immediately be explained as the same biological person, incarnation, alternate reality, or explicit multiverse counterpart.

The stronger rule is:

> **Let the games recognize one another without forcing the player to study franchise ontology in order to understand each individual game.**

## Supplemental products

Optional commercial supplements fit the series especially well because Second Gate already values manuals, journals, creature notes, fake period artifacts, music, and visual production material.

Good candidates include:

- original soundtrack;
- digital art book;
- a more authored faux manual / Summoner's Journal publication that also functions as an art book;
- deluxe bundles combining base game and supplements at a modest premium.

Do not gate essential narrative conclusions or required mechanics behind these products.

Game-development **asset packs** are a related but distinct business. They target creators rather than ordinary Second Gate players and should normally remain part of the owner's asset-store business rather than being forced into the Steam DLC taxonomy. Released Gate games can serve as strong demonstrations of those assets and the author's production practice.

## Publisher stance

Self-publishing remains the default path.

A publisher becomes interesting when it materially solves a specific expensive problem such as:

- funding;
- console/platform work;
- QA;
- localization;
- creator/press reach;
- distribution or release operations.

Do not trade control merely for external validation.

## Scope-control consequence

A desirable mechanic, renderer feature, minigame, setting, or narrative obsession does not automatically belong in Second Gate merely because Thestra can support it.

A legitimate answer is:

> **That is a future Gate idea.**

The series provides somewhere for artistic drift to go without requiring the current game to absorb every future possibility.
