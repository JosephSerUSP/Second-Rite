# Characters and creatures

## Individuality before taxonomy

Second Gate's spirits should feel encountered, named, remembered, and contracted as individuals rather than processed through an engineered monster taxonomy.

Creature names should sound as though they could have accumulated through folklore, translation, field notes, contact, or local usage. Prefer familiar folkloric/demonological roots, softened or feminized variants where appropriate, direct descriptive names, and only occasional invented roots.

Avoid pseudo-scientific stitched Latin/Greek naming as a default, lore-dump epithets, and overly cute naming that makes the roster feel like a collectible product line rather than a strange population.

## Species name and proper name

A creature can have a recognizable kind/species identity while an individual spirit carries a proper name. That distinction supports both gameplay legibility and attachment: the player can understand what sort of being they are dealing with while still remembering the particular individual who joined, survived, or was lost.

## Roster design

Roster expansion should be driven by **distinctive relationships and play roles**, not a target count alone. A new creature earns its place when it creates a new formation/combat/resource relationship, a memorable visual/narrative presence, or both.

Creature identity can emerge from:
- silhouette and portrait treatment;
- elemental/tactical role;
- skills and conditional behavior;
- Summoner/resource implications;
- temperament or autonomous behavior;
- food/item relationships;
- authored encounters, histories, reactions, or losses.

No single field needs to carry all of this, but a creature that differs only by numbers is not doing enough design work.

## Opening anchor

**Saban** is the opening attachment anchor: already contracted, present in the assigned room, and positioned as companion/carrier rather than a starter-monster selection screen. That relationship should teach the player how the game wants spirits to be perceived before the roster becomes large.

## Powerful spirits

A high-value spirit can be desirable partly because taking it changes expedition safety. Cerberus is the durable example: power should be able to shrink the player's safe margin rather than functioning as a strictly dominant upgrade.

## Roster scale and tiers

Historical roster proposals, tier tables, stat budgets, growth values, and exact actor lists are preserved as provenance, not live numeric authority. Current definitions live in Project data. Use the [legacy repo-design archive](archive/legacy-repo-design/README.md) to recover old rationale, then verify concrete facts against `data/` and current Issues.
