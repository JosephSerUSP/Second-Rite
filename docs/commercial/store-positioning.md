# Store Positioning — Working Copy

## One-sentence pitch

**Second Gate** is a first-person summoner dungeon RPG about descending beneath
remote St. Maria with contracted spirits, deciding how much danger and resource
pressure you can afford, and bringing home the companions you can.

## Short description

Enter the Labyrinth beneath St. Maria in a dark first-person turn-based RPG.
Contract strange spirits, build a party around their strengths and costs, push
for one more room or one more reward, and learn when to turn back before the
journey home becomes the dangerous part.

## Positioning principle

Sell **attachment, danger, strange beauty and consequence** before explaining
internal economy terminology.

Second Gate is not best introduced as a pile of searchable mechanics. The store
page should first make a player understand:

- there is a remote town called St. Maria;
- the player is a Summoner entering a forbidden/strange Labyrinth;
- spirits are persistent companions rather than anonymous battle tokens;
- stronger parties and tempting discoveries can make expedition logistics
  harder;
- coming home matters because the town and the party remember what happened;
- the deeper dungeon becomes increasingly incompatible with a simple history of
  the world.

## Long-description structure

1. **St. Maria** — Arrive as an outsider, register to cross the gate, prepare in
   a small town of people who have built lives around the Labyrinth.
2. **Pacts** — Travel with individually persistent contracted spirits whose
   battle roles and resource demands shape what an expedition can safely do.
3. **Descent** — Explore the Labyrinth in first person, fight turn-based battles,
   discover authored rooms/events, and choose when another branch is worth the
   cost.
4. **Return** — The path home is part of the game. Resources, losses, recruits
   and distance change what a safe return looks like.
5. **Memory** — St. Maria reacts to who returned, what was found, and what was
   lost. The deeper strata increasingly make ordinary archaeological explanations
   fail.

## What not to make carry the pitch

The following terms may be useful when truthful, but should not be the first
thing the player has to understand:

- **procedural generation**;
- **creature collector**;
- **permadeath**;
- **sacrifice**;
- **roguelite**.

Creature collection is a discoverability/tagging aid, not a sufficient emotional
identity. Permanent loss and ritual sacrifice may be important systems, but the
stronger public sentence is about whether the companions you brought into the
Labyrinth make it home.

Avoid leading with **roguelite** while the shipped structure does not match the
persistent meta-progression expectations players commonly attach to that term.

## Trailer thesis

Target roughly **45–60 seconds**. Gameplay begins immediately.

A strong first trailer should communicate a complete causal chain:

1. **Arrival** — carriage / remote St. Maria / the gate exists for Summoners.
2. **Saban** — a named persistent companion already waiting in the assigned
   room.
3. **Preparation** — Registry, Crossing Writ, supplies/equipment/party state.
4. **Descent** — first-person movement and strong dungeon atmosphere.
5. **Battle** — readable turn-based spirit combat with the actual game UI.
6. **Temptation** — Cerberus or another high-value opportunity that visibly
   changes the resource picture.
7. **Return pressure** — enough information for the viewer to understand that
   going farther threatens the journey home.
8. **Aftermath** — a changed room, ledger line, conversation, or other evidence
   that St. Maria remembers an expedition.
9. **Mystery** — one authored image/line suggesting that the Labyrinth is more
   than an ordinary buried ruin.
10. **End card** — SECOND GATE / Coming Soon / wishlist.

Do **not** reveal the São Paulo Metro stratum in the default first trailer. Its
commercial value currently lies partly in the shock of discovering a completely
recognizable contemporary place beneath a world that cannot name it.

## Capsule direction

At thumbnail size the capsule should communicate:

- one strong Summoner/human focal presence or silhouette;
- one memorable spirit rather than a tiny ensemble roster;
- descent / gate / depth / ritual imagery;
- dark PSX-era atmosphere without becoming visually muddy;
- a legible **SECOND GATE** title.

Avoid a generic castle, a wall of tiny characters, or an invented-word-first
composition that requires prior lore to parse.

## Public content pillars

### 1. Expedition stories

Concrete situations sell the game better than abstract systems:

- “Cerberus is much stronger than Saban. Taking him also destroys my safe
  distance home.”
- “I had enough strength for another branch. I wasn't sure I had enough to
  return.”
- “This companion died. St. Maria noticed.”
- “The room was different when I came back.”

### 2. St. Maria

Show specific people, rooms, services, objects and changed states rather than
posting broad lore summaries. The town is the social memory that gives a dungeon
run consequence.

### 3. Imagined lost-game artifacts

- Summoner notes and creature records;
- fake manual fragments;
- magazine-like screenshots/copy;
- intentional faux-localization instruction material;
- artbook / Summoner's Journal material that may later support a paid
  publication product.

These artifacts create identity, but should routinely point back to footage and
the wishlist path.

### 4. The wider Gate corpus

After Second Gate launches and the next title is concrete enough to announce,
recurring beings, places, music, superbosses and impossible guest encounters can
make the games advertise one another *inside the artwork* as well as through
store/franchise surfaces.

Do not make players understand a franchise timeline in order to understand
Second Gate by itself.

## Protected surprises

Some material is commercially valuable precisely because it is not in the store
page.

Current protected example:

- the **São Paulo Metro stratum**.

Marketing may later reveal protected material deliberately if evidence shows the
conversion value exceeds the cost to discovery, but do not expose it by default
just because it produces striking screenshots.
