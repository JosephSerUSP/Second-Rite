1) Central design thesis
- Turn Second Gate’s expedition economy into a debt/ledger story where MP, gold, contracts and names are all currencies — then force you to decide what to pay with. The Rusted Choir explicitly “takes its fee out of the summoning” (-25 MP) for greed; the Weighing Room prices money, a creature, or your name (“NOT YOURS TO WEIGH. YOU ARE HOLDING IT FOR SOMEONE.”); and the Third Bell literalises the ledger: “A name goes in with the clapper. … Whoever is named is what the bell means.”

2) Strongest three moments/ideas
- The Vigil actually answering back: “After the final name, something beneath St. Maria rings a third time. Every flame bends toward the gate. One extinguished lantern relights by itself.” Agnes’ commissioning lines seal the premise: “You are the only person … who can go and look, and the only person nobody will miss.”
- The Rusted Choir push‑your‑luck ladder that literally bills your walk home. First bell calms your party and grants XP; second spits “small denominations, St. Maria mint” and tilts the floor (party damage); third answers in a village accent with your name and charges you 25 MP: “The Choir takes its fee out of the summoning.”
- The Weighing Room’s moral/economic appraisal. Three clean, legible outcomes that hit theme and system at once: gold to full MP + Ether Seed with the killer line “quietly appalled”; name-for-MP+Seed+leaf that “weighs about as much as a coat”; creature rejected with chalked verdict “NOT YOURS TO WEIGH. YOU ARE HOLDING IT FOR SOMEONE.”

3) Weakest three moments/ideas
- Unplayed balance as design bet. The Eternal Warden is explicitly authored to “run long enough to reach Battle Strain” and “punish a party that arrived with no MP left,” but “has never been fought” (VALIDATION). This could collapse either way, undermining the finale.
- Optional rooms can just not exist for a run in practice. Floors 3–6 place authored rooms via “spawn: Random” and the README admits “guaranteed a legal position, not a findable one.” The campaign’s best ideas can simply be missed.
- The reward math can trivialise the horizon you’re trying to price. A -25 MP hit in the Choir is countered by a full MP fill in the Weighing Room plus Ether Seeds; Alicia now sells Town Portal and Bell Salt. The README itself flags “Whether that nets out to real expedition tension or trivialises it is unknown.”

4) Where pacing is most likely to collapse
- Act II on Floor 3–4: hunting for The Rusted Choir and The Weighing Room on procedural layouts that “expect some walking” risks long dead air, especially if the Choir spawns deep and you eat the -25 MP plus a fight far from stairs. Compounded by the compressed ramp (“roughly one incursion under‑levelled”) and the “Red Dragon holds the descent” gating callout. If the Weighing Room doesn’t spawn nearby to refill, the loop can stall; if it does, it can flatten tension.

5) What the owner will most likely name as their favourite
- The ending coda that cashes every optional flag into bespoke lines. They call out in VALIDATION that “Fifteen authored flags … were made load‑bearing.” The reflections pay off the Choir (“the clean bell … knew your name”), the Weighing Room (“quietly appalled”), the Garden leaf, the second bowl in Room 3, even Cerberus (“worth exactly none of it, and you would not give him back.”). That’s clearly their proudest systemic writing trick.

6) What the owner will most likely complain about
- The engine cannot actually end the game. They underline this as a Thestra gap: “A campaign cannot end… Every authored ending … must currently dead‑end into a map.” They built an epilogue St. Maria as a workaround and explicitly label this “the one thing here worth an Issue.”

7) What reads generic RPG versus specifically Second Gate
- Generic:
  - A big final guardian with adds and a relight (“SPAWN_ENEMIES” of a wisp troop) and HP‑threshold phase shocks; loot-as-large-gold payout (“GAIN_GOLD 4200”) for the creature‑naming ending.
  - The classic “greed room” (five bells ascend in reward and risk) and “weighing scale” puzzle room tropes, plus a Red Dragon blocking descent.
- Specifically Second Gate:
  - Explicit MP/MPD economy driving fiction: “The Choir takes its fee out of the summoning,” the Weighing Room refilling MP in exchange for ledger stakes, Agnes warning “a portal is a loan, and something below keeps the books.”
  - Contract/name metaphysics tied to St. Maria’s ledger and the Garden Without Wind’s paper leaves (“THE THIRD BELL RINGS FOR WHOEVER IS NAMED. NOBODY HAS BEEN NAMED FOR ELEVEN YEARS.”).
  - Town integration and Vigil cadence (two bells named; the third answers); Saban as your oldest contract; Ines’ blue chalk mark and the Half‑Contract; the one mandatory return beat adding a second feed bowl in Room 3.

8) One thing worth stealing into canon
- The Rusted Choir’s MP‑priced greed ladder that debits your walk home. It’s perfectly on‑brand for Second Gate’s “every step costs” identity; the -25 MP fee paired with immediate, ambiguous payout and a post‑greed fight is a great mechanical story beat.

9) One thing to absolutely leave experimental
- The Third Bell ledger of names and the ending tri‑choice to name yourself, name a creature (and get paid “promptly … for a name in good condition”), or cut the rope. “A name goes in with the clapper” reframes the world’s metaphysics and the Vigil; it’s a fantastic what‑if, but too determinative for canon.
