# Proof Build Brief

## Question

Can a new player enter St. Maria, become attached to at least one specific spirit,
understand that dungeon power competes with the ability to get home, and finish
one return wanting to descend again?

This proof tests **Second Gate as a commercial game**, not Thestra as a general
authoring system.

## Non-negotiable player arc

1. Arrive in St. Maria with enough authored context to understand that the
   Summoner is an outsider pursuing money/power rather than prophecy.
2. Meet the already-contracted **Saban** in the assigned room.
3. Register with Celina and receive the Crossing Writ.
4. Make at least one practical preparation choice before descent.
5. Physically approach and cross the gate into the Labyrinth.
6. Experience first-person exploration and at least one real battle.
7. Encounter one strong temptation that changes expedition safety, preferably
   the authored Cerberus contract or an equivalent high-cost opportunity.
8. Reach an understandable **push deeper vs. return** decision.
9. Return to St. Maria or fail in a way the player can explain.
10. See an authored aftermath: changed dialogue, room state, spirit reaction,
    discovery report, or another consequence that proves the return matters.
11. End on a mystery/possibility that makes another incursion desirable.

## Scope

### St. Maria

- Passage House / assigned room with Saban.
- Passage Office / Registry / Crossing Writ.
- Enough of the physical town to establish St. Maria as a place rather than a
  menu shell.
- At least two optional services or interactions that create a meaningful
  preparation choice.
- At least one return-state reaction after the dungeon portion.

### Dungeon

The proof does **not** need to represent the final full-game floor count.
It should provide enough contiguous dungeon play to prove:

- the first-person presentation;
- navigation and retracing;
- encounter/battle readability;
- shared Summoner-resource pressure;
- one tempting optional commitment/reward;
- one understandable reason to stop pushing and go home;
- one authored mystery/discovery rather than only generated geometry.

The São Paulo Metro stratum is **not** required for the proof build and should
remain protected as a later-game discovery.

### Spirit set

Use enough spirits to demonstrate distinct expedition roles without turning the
proof into a roster-completion exercise.

Required proof roles include:

- **Saban** as the efficient named opening companion;
- **Cerberus** or an equivalent immediately exciting high-cost power option;
- at least one additional role that makes party composition visibly different
  rather than merely changing damage numbers.

Spirit identity should be legible through name, art/presentation, skills,
resource consequences and authored reactions/history where available.

### Required systems

Only systems necessary to make the above arc honest are proof-build blockers.
At minimum:

- save/load safety sufficient for external playtests;
- Registry/Crossing Writ progression;
- party/contract state;
- first-person dungeon movement and interaction;
- encounter and battle flow;
- shared Summoner MP/resource presentation;
- persistent spirit identity;
- loss/emergency continuation behavior where the current design exposes it;
- physical return or a clearly authored legal return method;
- changed St. Maria state after the expedition.

Do not block the proof build on unrelated Thestra generalization merely because
the engine could eventually support it.

## Test questions

Ask after play without leading the answer:

1. What were you trying to do when you entered the Labyrinth?
2. Who or what do you remember most from St. Maria?
3. Which spirit do you remember, and why?
4. Was there a point where you considered turning back? What caused it?
5. What did taking or refusing the strongest opportunity cost you?
6. Why did your expedition end the way it did?
7. What changed when you returned?
8. What do you think is wrong with the Labyrinth?
9. Would you make another incursion right now? Why or why not?

## Green-light evidence

The proof is working when most testers independently communicate some version of:

1. **“I want to go down there again.”**
2. **“I cared about / had a plan for that spirit.”**
3. **“I understood that going deeper or taking more power could make getting home harder.”**
4. **“St. Maria or the Labyrinth feels like something I want to understand.”**

Players do not need to describe exact formulas, economy terminology, or hidden
implementation rules for the design to count as legible.

## Failure diagnoses

| Observation | Likely problem | First response |
|---|---|---|
| Players treat St. Maria as menu downtime | Hub lacks physical/authored consequence | Strengthen spatial identity and return-state reactions before adding more NPC quantity |
| Nobody remembers Saban or another spirit | Spirit identity is not surfacing | Improve individual presentation/history/utility rather than adding roster size |
| Cerberus/high-power choice is an automatic yes | Cost is not concrete enough | Make expedition-range/resource consequence immediately legible |
| Players never consider returning | Danger/return value is unreadable or under-rewarded | Improve forecast, route-back cues and escalating resource pressure |
| Players return only because content obviously ends | Proof build is teaching demo boundaries rather than game judgment | Put a real optional temptation before the return decision |
| Players fail without knowing why | Information/UI problem, not productive difficulty | Improve logs, forecast, warnings and state visibility |
| Return to town feels like a reset | No authored aftermath | Add restrained dialogue/room/service state that remembers the expedition |
| Players finish but do not want another incursion | Mystery/decision density is too weak | Improve the specific remembered moments before enlarging scope |

## Demo relationship

The eventual public demo may grow from this proof, but the two are not identical.

The public demo should be a polished **25–45 minute sales argument** with a
satisfying miniature arc and wishlist path. The private proof may be rougher and
shorter so long as it answers the questions above honestly.
