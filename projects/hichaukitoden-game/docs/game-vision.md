# Game vision

## Identity

**Second Gate** is a dark, PSX-influenced first-person summoner dungeon RPG about descending beneath remote St. Maria with contracted spirits, deciding how much danger and resource pressure an expedition can afford, and living with who makes it back.

The player is an outsider pursuing money and power rather than answering a prophecy. St. Maria is not a menu shell around the dungeon: it is an inhabited authored place whose rooms, services, relationships, and reactions preserve the memory of expeditions.

## Core emotional promise

The center of the game is **attachment under logistical pressure**.

Spirits are persistent contracted beings with individual identities, not disposable collection tokens. Stronger companions and stronger commitments should create concrete tradeoffs. The intended tension is not simply “can I win the next battle?” but also “what does this choice do to the expedition, the return, and the people I am trying to bring home?”

The return journey matters as much as the descent. Retreat should be an authored strategic choice rather than a failure to keep exploring, and returning to St. Maria should create aftermath rather than merely refill resources.

## Place, memory, and mystery

The Labyrinth should become less explainable as the player descends. Early material may support ordinary archaeological or historical readings; later strata should resist a single explanation and suggest recurrence, memory, and incompatible histories.

The São Paulo Metro stratum is a current example of that principle and should normally remain a protected discovery rather than default promotional imagery. See [Strata and return](world/strata-and-return.md).

Second Gate may support a surface ending and a deeper, deliberately non-obvious route that meaningfully reframes the work without requiring guide-only obscurity. Exact ending conditions are authored content and should not be invented here.

## Spirits and the Summoner

The Summoner is primarily a commander/support driver rather than another conventional damage dealer. Party composition, contracted spirits, formation, resources, and expedition commitments are the main field of agency. See [Summoning and expedition](gameplay/summoning-and-expedition.md) and [Characters and creatures](characters-and-creatures.md).

## Combat and material play

Combat should make creature roles, elemental commitments, resource pressure, and composition legible without reducing elements to a universal rock-paper-scissors table. See [Combat](gameplay/combat.md).

Food, items, equipment, and crafting should reinforce preparation, discovery, recovery, and creature individuality rather than exist as disconnected inventory breadth. See [Items and crafting](gameplay/items-and-crafting.md).

## Visual and cultural position

Second Gate should feel like a strange late-1990s/early-2000s console RPG remembered through another culture rather than a generic modern “retro” pastiche. Low-poly first-person space, period-shaped RPG interfaces, sparse expressive character art, unusual material choices, and deliberate awkwardness may coexist when they remain comprehensible and specific.

The work is made from a Brazilian perspective shaped by encountering Japanese games, music, fantasy, and localization from outside Japan and the anglophone center. Queer experience may live in relationships and world texture without becoming a compulsory marketing category. See [Art direction](art-direction.md).

## Authorship boundary

Player-facing Second Gate is an authored game. Agentic/LLM-generated Projects, scenes, campaign interpretations, stress tests, and RTP experiments may be valuable development evidence, but they do not become player-facing Second Gate content merely because they were useful during development.

## Documentation role

This document records durable **game intent and meaning**, not implementation status. Concrete authored state lives in the Project's `data/` and `assets/`; unresolved implementation or tuning work belongs in GitHub Issues. Do not copy a stale number or old implementation sentence into this page to make it look settled.
