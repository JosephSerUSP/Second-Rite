# Studio splash visual study

This study focuses on **editor startup splash artwork**: the image shown while the RPG Maker application itself launches. It does not use the map editor workspace or the splash/title sequence shown when launching a game.

## Reference collection

The most complete reference found is a community-assembled, PC-only collection. Its author says it contains 30 splash screens from RPG Maker 95 through MV, across Japanese, English, and French releases, with some variants still missing. It includes official releases and unofficial translations, so treat it as a visual archive rather than a canonical list of shipped editions.

## Selected direction

Use the dragon crossing an oversized moon above the ruined observatory, with a four-person ensemble, as the startup splash illustration. Keep the horizontal framing, pencil contours, and matte gouache fills; use charcoal plum, pale bone, and muted rose rather than the earlier blue-and-yellow pairing. The first ensemble redesign is the selected draft because it best preserves the original framing while distinguishing the travelers through their silhouettes and props.

## What the splash art is doing

Across the older desktop versions, the image works like compact fantasy key art: a party, a striking landscape, or both. The RPG Maker name and version are part of the composition, while the illustration sells the promise of making an adventure. The 2000/2003 runtime logos are a separate category: those appear when a *game* starts, before its title screen. [EasyRPG documents that distinction and the version differences](https://blog.easyrpg.org/2025/03/a-collection-of-rpg-maker-2000-2003-bootleg-logos/).

The splashes vary by version and localization. The archive is useful for studying shifts in illustration, logo placement, and mood; it does not support treating one image as the universal RPG Maker look.

## Moon-led landscape directions

The next pass changes both the composition and the drawing treatment. The format is horizontal, the moon is a dominant shape, and the scene should feel like a specific late-80s/90s fantasy OVA or console-RPG moment rather than a generic party portrait. The requested visual territory is closer to *Record of Lodoss War* and *Valkyrie Profile*: expressive hand-drawn character art, strong silhouettes, and a mythic, slightly tragic mood. These are broad references, not character or image reproductions.

The owner preferred the original dragon-crossing composition, while finding its generic party design and blue-and-yellow palette weak. The following refinements retain its framing and moon/dragon/observatory hierarchy, use darker plum and bone with rose accents, and draw from the game's specific Summoner-and-Saban relationship.

### Summoner and Saban: cel palette

A travel-worn Summoner stands with Saban, the peculiar pink mount bird, rather than a lineup of generic fantasy classes. The dark plum sky, bone moon, and muted rose accents remove the blue-and-yellow pairing.

![Summoner and Saban cel palette](assets/splash-dragon-specific-01-cel-plum-rose.png)

A flatter poster-paint pass using charcoal, faded mauve, rose, and pale moon tones.

## Dragon-crossing variations

The dragon flying across the moon remains the anchor. This pass holds that moment and the observatory party constant while varying framing, drawing material, and palette.

### Off-center moon, cel ink, and vermilion

The moon sits upper-left; the dragon cuts diagonally toward the party at lower-right. Clean contours and hard cel shadows use a cream, navy, and vermilion palette.

![Off-center moon and cel ink](assets/splash-dragon-crossing-01-ova-cel.png)

### Right-weighted moon, pencil and gouache

The observatory and party anchor the left, while the dragon crosses a large moon on the right. The palette shifts toward teal and plum with opaque matte fills.

![Pencil and gouache dragon study](assets/splash-dragon-crossing-02-pencil-gouache.png)

### Close dragon, pencil and marker

The dragon moves into the foreground, with its head and leading wing enlarged against the moon and the party reduced at lower-left. Cool gray, violet, celadon, and oxblood shift the palette.

![Pencil and marker dragon study](assets/splash-dragon-crossing-03-pencil-marker.png)

### Wide silhouette, pen and poster paint

The moon and dragon take the left half; the party and observatory sit to the right. Broad flat areas and crisp edges give this the most poster-like staging.

![Pen and poster paint dragon study](assets/splash-dragon-crossing-04-pen-poster.png)

The compositions vary clearly. The material treatments are less distinct than intended: the generator kept returning to a similar outlined cel look, and the moon retains some surface mottling. Treat these as layout and palette studies for now.

## Same composition, different materials

To compare the media more directly, these keep a consistent moon, flight path, party, and observatory arrangement, with the drawing treatment and palette as the main variables.

### Cel animation

Ink contours, flat cel colors, and hard shadow shapes in ivory, navy, and brick red.

![Cel animation material study](assets/splash-dragon-material-01-cel.png)

### Pencil and gouache

Graphite contours with matte opaque paint, using a quieter teal, plum, coral, and gold palette. The output leans into visible paint texture.

![Pencil and gouache material study](assets/splash-dragon-material-02-gouache.png)

### Pencil and marker

Graphite contour work and marker-like color overlaps, with cool gray-violet and celadon. This one also kept noticeable surface mottling in the moon.

![Pencil and marker material study](assets/splash-dragon-material-03-marker.png)

### Pen and poster paint

Large flat shapes and a reduced navy, ivory, and vermilion palette give the clearest graphic treatment.

![Pen and poster paint material study](assets/splash-dragon-material-04-poster.png)

The cel and poster-paint versions read cleanest. The gouache and marker versions show more surface variation than the requested clean finish. None is final artwork.

The dragon crossing the moon is the selected starting point for further iteration. These keep the wide moon-and-observatory setting and test three different dragon beats, with cleaner line and color shapes as the target.

These studies vary the drawing and paint media. The target is tidy outlines and deliberate broad color shapes without a noise overlay or faux-paper texture.
